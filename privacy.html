Daiboju disaster alerts

Daiboju sends disaster alerts for Japan. It is not an official warning service. When you sign up, it stores only what is needed to send you alerts: the delivery channel you chose, your contact for that channel (browser push subscription, email address, phone number or LINE user ID), your language, your prefecture, whether you also want drill messages, and the sign-up time. The contact is stored encrypted. Daiboju has no accounts and does not ask for your name.

Your exact location is never sent to Daiboju. The next-steps page finds evacuation sites on your phone; to do so, your phone downloads public map data for its area from the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (GSI), which can show GSI your approximate area (about 40 km). The guidance written with Google Gemini receives only official hazard data and the prefecture, never your contact or location.

Daiboju runs on Google Cloud Run in Tokyo. Depending on the channel you choose, alerts are delivered through your browser's push service, Resend (email), LINE, or Twilio (SMS and WhatsApp), which may process your contact outside Japan under their own terms. Your record is kept until you delete it with the link shown after sign-up or in alert messages, and is removed automatically when a channel reports the address no longer exists. While the service is in test mode, alerts are simulated and records may be cleared when the service restarts.
